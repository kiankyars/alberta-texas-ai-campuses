# Temple AI Campus — Texas development dossier

**Concept / pre-feasibility proposal · 13 October 2026 · native USD · 100 MW IT in four 25 MW phases**

This is an independently scoped facility-development project. It is not a Texas benchmark for another project. The deliverable is a candidate-site investigation, technical concept, auditable economic case and executable sequence of diligence gates. It does not constitute land control, utility approval, a permit, a construction design or an investment commitment.

## Investment decision

**Advance a capped diligence program; do not authorize construction or promise an energization date.** Base-case equity returns also miss the proposed investment hurdle, so regulatory reopening alone would not justify FID. The North Temple industrial area is worth investigating for a water-conserving, liquid-cooled wholesale AI campus. A defensible commitment requires a real parcel, 140 MW peak power pathway, regulatory reopening, demonstrated thermal performance and a credit-supported anchor lease.

The controlling current fact is the Texas approval hold. The Governor's September 21 directive and TCEQ's current construction-stormwater notice prevent treating ordinary permitting times as a live schedule. TCEQ explicitly includes construction general-permit applications for data-center projects. The proposed calendar therefore contains an unknown period before **M0**, followed by a conditional 48-month first-phase path. October 19 is a requested government compliance update, not a promised reopening. [TX05](https://gov.texas.gov/news/post/governor-abbott-directs-tceq-to-halt-data-center-permits) [TX06](https://www.tceq.texas.gov/permitting/stormwater/construction/)

The asset earns capacity rent and recovers metered electricity costs. It does not purchase GPUs or sell inference/training tokens. No income is assumed from energy arbitrage, generator dispatch, tax incentives or speculative land appreciation.

## Product and technical basis

Deliver four independently commissioned 25 MW IT blocks. The design target is concurrent maintenance of critical distribution and cooling, subject to a failure-mode study; no certification or availability percentage is claimed. Customer equipment and upstream network fabrics lie outside the landlord's capital boundary. A customer interface schedule must fix rack density, power quality, liquid temperatures/chemistry, maximum step load, rack weights, white-space scope and commissioning loads.

Capacity definitions must remain distinct:

- 100 MW IT is the campus's contracted design boundary.
- Annual PUE 1.28 is a planning estimate for energy accounting.
- Peak PUE 1.40 implies a preliminary **140 MW facility demand** at full IT design load.
- 90% committed occupancy is commercial utilization of built IT capacity.
- 85% IT load factor is average metered draw on that committed capacity.

At stabilized base assumptions, average IT draw is 76.5 MW and average facility draw is 97.92 MW. Annual purchased electricity is approximately **857,779 MWh** before further refinements to seasonal and fixed-load modeling. At USD80/MWh it costs approximately **USD68.62 million/year**, recovered as a separate electricity pass-through. It is not landlord operating profit. An actual tariff with demand charges needs an interval-load model; an all-in MWh allowance is an underwriting simplification.

The detailed electrical and thermal quantities belong to the shared engineering worksheet and accompanying concept drawings. They must be reconciled before procurement. The electrical concept uses a provisional 138 kV intake, an N+1 transformer capacity arrangement, sectional medium-voltage distribution, block UPS and emergency standby sized for design cooling auxiliaries as well as IT. Final voltage, fault duty, protection settings and grounding depend on the utility study. Dual switchboards do not establish independent utility supply.

A minimum 100 MW energy balance flows through the tenant equipment. At the assumed 90% direct-liquid capture, 90 MW goes to the liquid circuit and 10 MW remains for room-air cooling, before pumping, UPS and refrigeration heat. At a 10 K liquid temperature difference, 90 MW corresponds to roughly 2,150 kg/s water-equivalent circulation. This is **recirculated heat-transfer flow**, not daily water consumption.

### Power pathway

Submit one disclosed full-campus demand/ramp. A four-phase build does not create four independent exemptions from large-load rules. The project's expected facility demand exceeds ERCOT's 75 MW large-load threshold. It has no existing queue position or demonstrated Batch Zero eligibility; the published July 2026 intake dates have passed. The first task is obtaining the applicable post-hold entry route from the interconnecting utility/ERCOT, not assuming historic serial-queue treatment. [TX04](https://www.ercot.com/services/rq/large-load-integration/)

The adopted **16 TAC §25.194, effective October 8, 2026**, requires material commercial/technical disclosures and financial commitments. At 140 MW requested peak, the stated USD50,000/MW intermediate security basis yields **USD7 million**, with a **USD100,000 study fee**. Final security can be higher because it is tied to the greater of the prescribed per-MW amount and allocated upgrade costs. Confirm all tariff, credit, milestone and return/forfeiture terms with counsel and the utility. Security is separately modeled liquidity, not a second copy of construction cost. [TX07, printed pages 6931–6940](https://www.sos.state.tx.us/texreg/pdf/backview/1002/1002is.pdf)

Required studies include steady-state and contingency power flow, short circuit, protection coordination, dynamic behavior/ride-through, harmonics and any subsynchronous issues. Model UPS and chiller response, IT load steps, fault recovery and restoration—not simply a flat MW number. Commission agreed telemetry, metering and protection and obtain explicit authorization before each energization stage.

Choose grid supply with emergency generators in the base case. A prime-power gas alternative is a separate project requiring firm gas supply and pressure, emissions/air authorization, operability and maintenance reserves, noise controls and finance. It is not a presumed route around the state data-center permit hold. Emergency equipment must not be underwritten for routine economic dispatch without explicit environmental and contractual permission.

### Cooling, weather and water

Use direct-to-chip CDUs and segregated secondary circuits, dry heat rejection and air-cooled mechanical trim, with independently maintainable pump and heat-rejection modules. Mechanical trim is essential when outdoor temperature exceeds the useful dry-cooler approach to the customer loop. An annual average temperature cannot size this system.

Use 43°C dry bulb as a provisional engineering screen and 46°C as a stress case, subject to local design-weather review and vendor curves. NWS's Waco record is a regional proxy showing both hot summers and freezes; it is not the Temple design weather file. Include hail/wind damage resistance, freeze protection, heat-trace needs and loss-of-cooling recovery. Validate annual PUE using an hourly simulation at customer-required liquid temperatures. [TX09](https://www.weather.gov/fwd/act_normals)

The base design has no evaporative cooling towers. Its initial domestic/service allowance is **15 m³/day**, approximately 5,475 m³/year, excluding separately estimated first fill and emergency fire events. It is not a municipal allocation. At the modeled full-campus average IT energy, that allowance would be roughly 0.008 L/kWh; this arithmetic is a scope-limited domestic-water indicator, not a measured campus WUE. Count all actual water sources, fill, makeup, discharge and construction use before making any efficiency claim. Obtain City service, fire-flow and drought terms; no groundwater wells or new water rights are assumed. TWDB's operational survey obligations are distinct from water service authorization. [TX08](https://www.twdb.texas.gov/waterplanning/waterusesurvey/digitalinfrastructure.asp)

Two physically independent fiber paths require traced carrier routes, separate site entrances, separated duct banks and restoration commitments. Two invoices or two carrier logos do not prove diversity. No route or latency claim is presently supported.

## Commercial and financial underwriting

Offer 15-year wholesale capacity leases with electricity billed separately on a defined metering/PUE basis. Target a creditworthy 50 MW anchor, then a second customer or anchor expansion for the remaining phases. No counterparties have been contacted and no demand is represented as secured.

The model horizon is 20 years from the first COD, but the proposed initial lease term is 15 years per phase. It therefore includes **60, 54, 48 and 42 months of uncontracted renewal/reletting revenue** for phases 1–4. That tail assumes unchanged rent and occupancy, no downtime and no renewal CAPEX. It is an explicit underwriting assumption, not part of the initial take-or-pay lease. A lender case must haircut or remove the tail and fund reletting costs; no present customer contract supports it.

Specify liquid temperature/chemistry and density limits, capacity ramp, acceptance conditions, utility delay relief, customer fit-out boundary, rent commencement, delivery long-stop date, curtailment terms and SLA remedies. Use a parent guarantee or acceptable bank security. A flexible training workload and a strict always-on inference SLA are different products; price them accordingly.

## Risks, gates and immediate diligence

The most material risks are the state hold, unknown power allocation/upgrades, a historic tract that may be unavailable, anchor credit/ramp, hot-weather performance, unconfirmed water/fire/fiber, generator authorization and equipment delivery. The machine-readable risk register assigns impacts, mitigation, triggers and accountable roles. None is eliminated by a polished model.

Gate order is: **G0 lawful restart → G1 site control → G2 power/water → G3 bankable demand → G4 FID → G5 staged energization → G6 accepted tenant handover.** Some evidence gathering runs in parallel, but capital release follows the relevant gates. An existing utility queue elsewhere or industrial site's advertised substation does not prove this project has secured capacity. [TX15](https://www.oncor.com/content/oncorwww/wire/en/home/newsroom/oncor-reports-first-quarter-2026-results.html)

For the next four weeks, prepare the following requests without implying they have been sent:

1. **Regulatory counsel:** current hold interpretation, lawful diligence boundary, post-hold large-load path and collateral obligations. Stop if the proposed route relies on an unconfirmed exemption.
2. **EDC/landowner:** parcel IDs, current availability, owner authority, survey, restrictions and conditional option terms. Replace the tract if it is unavailable or fails physical fit.
3. **DSP/TSP:** 140 MW peak request, four-stage IT ramp, product/UPS/generator profile, candidate POIs and study/security process. Require actual project evidence before a delivery promise.
4. **Civil/City:** flood, wetland, geotechnical and airport screening, land-use classification, municipal service/fire-flow and drought terms.
5. **OEMs/carriers/tenants:** comparable budget quotations, thermal curves, physical fiber routes and credit-supported customer requirements.

A sponsor could authorize an initial **USD250,000 desktop diligence cap** as an estimate, with separate approval for surveys/fieldwork and no nonrefundable land purchase or equipment order until the corresponding gate. A complete utility study and full pre-FID development effort require a much larger, separately approved budget plus security. These are proposed spending gates; no money has been committed.

Kill or replace the project if the land cannot be controlled; 140 MW cannot be connected on viable terms; the current approval pathway has no acceptable resolution at the sponsor's six-month review; a 50 MW creditworthy anchor cannot be contracted; or combined downside fails finance and return hurdles. Maintain the full 100 MW concept while releasing each next phase only against evidence.
